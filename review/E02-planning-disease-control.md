# Review Notes · E02 Planning, Implementing and Evaluating Disease Control

**Competencies:** CM 8.5

## Check Before Teaching

- **Park edition.** Definitions in quotation marks come from Park 27e Chapter 2 (concepts of control: disease control, elimination, eradication, monitoring, surveillance, evaluation) and Chapter 23 (planning cycle; objectives, targets and goals; steps and elements of evaluation), read in full from the split 27e set. Check against 28e if you teach from it.
- **Scope.** Health planning in India (Five Year Plans, NITI Aayog, planning cycle in depth) and management techniques are in Part K (K5, K6). This deck applies the cycle to disease control at block and village level.
- **Planning cycle (page 6).** A simplified eight-step version of Park's Fig. 1; the notes list Park's extra boxes.
- **Hanlon calculator (page 8).** Score = (A + 2B) × C ÷ 3 on 0–10 scales, with PEARL as a yes/no screen, as presented in the NACCHO Guide to Prioritization Techniques (Hanlon and Pickett, 1984). A search did not return the original 1984 text; the formula is the standard teaching version. Preset scores for hypertension, malaria and dengue are invented for the exercise.
- **SMART objectives (page 9).** The numbers in the improved objectives are illustrative.
- **Indicator chain (page 15).** Input, process, output, outcome and impact is a logic-model convention; Park's own evaluation types are structure, process and outcome (Donabedian), which the notes map onto it.
- **Epidemic threshold (page 16).** Mean + 2 SD of the same weeks in previous years is a generic, widely taught method (CDC). IDSP's own triggers include clusters of cases or deaths; check the current IDSP/IHIP guidance if you want to quote its exact rules.
- **Opening case (page 2).** Invented block figures. Replace them with your field-practice block if you have the data.
