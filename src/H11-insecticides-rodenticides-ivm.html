DDT: Hero and Villain

Action
  • Contact poison; enters through the feet, paralyses legs and wings
  • Kills slowly, over several hours; no repellent action
  • Residual action up to 18 months on good surfaces; less on mud walls
  • Residual spray: 100–200 mg per sq ft; dust 5–10% for lice, fleas, ticks
Controversy
  • Persists in the environment and food chains
  • Restricted worldwide under the Stockholm Convention, allowed only for disease-vector control
  • Widespread vector resistance in India; largely replaced by synthetic pyrethroids for IRS

Discovered as an insecticide by Paul Müller in 1939 (Nobel Prize, 1948).

0:15–0:20. Park quotes a WHO Expert Committee that saw no valid reason to stop DDT for house spraying on safety grounds; resistance and environmental concerns are why its use has fallen. Check your state's current IRS insecticide under NCVBDC.

The Rest of the Toolkit

Malathion

Least toxic organophosphate; residual spray every 3 months; ULV fogging in dengue and encephalitis outbreaks.

Temephos (Abate)

Larvicide for wells and domestic containers at not more than 1 ppm; low toxicity.

Pyrethrum

From Chrysanthemum flowers; kills instantly; an excellent space spray; no residual action.

Mineral Oils

Kerosene, malariol: a film suffocates larvae and pupae.

Repellents: diethyltoluamide (DEET), dimethyl phthalate, benzyl benzoate.

0:25–0:30. Synthetic pyrethroids are used for IRS and to treat bed nets (LLINs) because of low mammalian toxicity and rapid knock-down.

Resistance and Safety

Insecticide Resistance
  • Selection of insects that survive a dose that once killed them
  • Detect with WHO susceptibility tests
  • Manage by rotation, mixtures, mosaics and non-chemical methods
Protect the Spraymen and the Family
  • Masks, gloves, full clothing; wash after work; no eating during spraying
  • Cover food, water and utensils; keep children away
  • Organophosphate poisoning: atropine and pralidoxime

0:30–0:37. Answer the opening case: explain the smell is short-lived, ask families not to re-plaster for the season, and explain that resistance is monitored and insecticides rotated.

Rodent-borne Disease and Rodent Control

Diseases Linked to Rodents

Plague, leptospirosis, murine typhus, scrub typhus (via mites), rat-bite fever, salmonellosis, Lassa fever.

Methods
  • Environmental: rat-proof buildings and food stores; clear garbage and harbourage
  • Trapping
  • Rodenticides (see right)
  • Fumigation of burrows: aluminium phosphide, calcium cyanide

In a plague outbreak, kill the fleas first (insecticide dust in burrows and runways), then the rats. Otherwise fleas leave dead rats for humans.

Take-Home

  1. Four chemical groups: organochlorines, organophosphates, carbamates, pyrethroids.
  2. DDT: long residual action, but restricted and resisted.
  3. Temephos for water; malathion and pyrethrum for space sprays.
  4. Rotate insecticides; protect spraymen; atropine and pralidoxime for OP poisoning.
  5. IVM combines methods, sectors and communities.
