0:03–0:08. Faecal carriers are commoner than urinary carriers. A chronic carrier may excrete for life. Treatment of carriers: prolonged antibiotics; cholecystectomy if gall stones.

0:08–0:15. Water-borne outbreaks are explosive; food-borne outbreaks are often linked to one carrier.

Which Test, Which Week?

Bone marrow culture stays positive even after antibiotics. Treat with azithromycin or ceftriaxone, guided by local resistance.

0:15–0:23. A single Widal test in an endemic area is often misleading because of past infection and vaccination; a four-fold rise in paired sera is more meaningful. Fluoroquinolone resistance is widespread in South Asia; extensively drug-resistant (XDR) typhoid emerged in Pakistan in 2016.

Preventing Food Poisoning

Temperature

Keep cold food below 5 °C, hot food above 60 °C; reheat thoroughly

Food Handlers

Hand washing; no work with diarrhoea or skin sores; periodic medical checks

Kitchen

Separate raw and cooked; clean surfaces; safe water; pest control

The danger zone, 5–60 °C, is where bacteria multiply fastest. Large-batch cooking for events carries the highest risk.

0:46–0:50. Link to WHO's Five Keys to Safer Food: keep clean, separate raw and cooked, cook thoroughly, keep food at safe temperatures, use safe water and raw materials.
