A Newly Diagnosed Diabetic Asks, "Doctor, What Can I Eat?"

He is 48, a shopkeeper, BMI 27, BP 146/92. His wife cooks for a family of five on a tight budget.

Will a printed "diabetic diet chart" work for him?

0:00–0:03. A printed chart ignores the family pot, cost, local foods and habits. Good diet planning starts from what the family already eats.

Steps in Planning a Diet

  1. Assess: age, sex, activity, physiological state, illness, current diet (24-hour recall)
  2. Requirement: energy and protein from ICMR 2020; consumption units for the family
  3. Translate into food groups (My Plate quantities), not nutrients
  4. Adapt to local foods, season, cost, culture, religion and likes
  5. Distribute across meals; use household measures (katori, ladle, glass)
  6. Check and follow up: weight, symptoms, adherence

0:03–0:10. Balanced diet: one that contains a variety of foods in such quantities and proportions that the need for energy, nutrients and other components is adequately met, with a small provision for extra nutrients to withstand short periods of leanness.

Make It Cheaper, Keep It Balanced

0:10–0:16. Germination, fermentation and malting also improve nutritive value cheaply.

Diet in Type 2 Diabetes

  • Energy to reach and keep a healthy weight
  • Carbohydrate about 50–55% of energy, mainly from whole grains, millets and pulses
  • Prefer low glycaemic index foods; plenty of fibre and vegetables
  • Regular meals; avoid long fasts if on insulin or sulfonylureas
  • Limit sugar, sweets, refined flour (maida), fruit juices
  • Whole fruit in moderate portions
  • Fat ≤ 30% of energy; avoid trans fats
  • Protein from pulses, low-fat milk, eggs, fish

Plate method: half the plate non-starchy vegetables, a quarter whole grains, a quarter pulses or other protein.

0:16–0:24. Check the percentages against the RSSDI guideline your medicine department follows.

Hypertension: How Much Salt Did He Eat?

Salt Today

Red line: 5 g a day (WHO)

0:24–0:31. Salt values are typical estimates for teaching, not lab values. DASH-style diet: fruits, vegetables, low-fat dairy, whole grains, nuts; less salt, saturated fat and sugar. Indians eat roughly 8–11 g of salt a day in surveys.

Obesity: A Realistic Target

  • Cut 500 kcal a day, mostly from oil, sugar and refined snacks
  • Add 150+ minutes of moderate activity a week
  • Aim for 5–10% weight loss first
Expected Fat Loss

0:31–0:37. The 7,700 kcal/kg rule overestimates long-term loss because energy expenditure adapts; use it only to set expectations. Crash diets under 1,000 kcal need medical supervision.

What Must Be on the Label

Identity

Name of the food; list of ingredients in descending order of weight; allergens

Nutrition Information

Per 100 g or 100 ml and per serve: energy, protein, carbohydrate (total and added sugars), total fat (saturated and trans fat), sodium

Other

Veg (green) or non-veg (brown) logo; FSSAI licence number; date of manufacture and "use by"; net quantity; "+F" for fortified foods

0:37–0:42. Front-of-pack warning labels (for high fat, sugar, salt) have been proposed by FSSAI but check their current status before teaching.

Take-Home

  1. Plan from the family pot: local, affordable, acceptable foods.
  2. Diabetes: whole grains, low GI, half the plate vegetables.
  3. Hypertension: salt under 5 g; more fruits and vegetables.
  4. Obesity: about 500 kcal/day less and more activity.
  5. Read the label: first ingredient, added sugar, saturated fat, sodium.
