Mid-upper Arm Circumference: One Tape

  • For children 6–59 months; measure the left arm, midway between acromion and olecranon
  • MUAC stays fairly constant from 1 to 5 years, so no age is needed
  • Used by ASHAs and AWWs for community screening

0:26–0:30. Older age-independent cut-off (Park): 13.5 cm and above normal; 12.5–13.5 mild-moderate; below 12.5 severe. The SAM/MAM cut-offs on the slide are the WHO 2009 standards in current Indian guidelines.

Dietary Assessment: Three Methods

1

Weighment

Weigh all raw food cooked by the family over 1–21 days. Accurate but needs time and co-operation.

2

Oral Questionnaire (24-h Recall)

Recall everything eaten in the last 24 hours, with standard cups and spoons. Quick; relies on memory.

3

Log Book

The family records food used. Needs literacy and motivation.

Also: food frequency questionnaire for habitual intake. Family intake is converted to per-head intake using consumption units.

0:38–0:44. Practical tip for family visits: use standard household measures (katori, glass, ladle) and the NIN "Nutritive Value of Indian Foods" or the Indian Food Composition Tables (IFCT 2017) to calculate intake.

Take-Home

  1. Assess with ABCD: anthropometric, biochemical, clinical, dietary.
  2. Stunting = chronic; wasting = acute; underweight = both.
  3. MUAC below 11.5 cm (6–59 months) = severe acute malnutrition.
  4. Indian adults: overweight from BMI 23, obese from 25.
  5. 24-hour recall: quick, cheap, and good enough to count food groups.
