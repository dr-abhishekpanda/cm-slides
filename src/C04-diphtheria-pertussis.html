Diphtheria: A Disease of Missed Vaccination

AgentCorynebacterium diphtheriae; exotoxin from toxigenic strains (gravis, intermedius, mitis)
ReservoirCases and carriers (far more numerous)
SpreadDroplets; contact with skin lesions; fomites
Incubation2–6 days
InfectiousUsually 14–28 days; carriers longer
Who Gets It Now

Older children and adolescents, as immunity from childhood doses wanes without boosters. Outbreaks in India where coverage is low.

0:03–0:10. Park: carriers outnumber cases (about 95 carriers to 5 cases in some estimates); nasal carriers are more dangerous than throat carriers. Faucial (tonsillar) diphtheria is the commonest form.

The Toxin Travels: Spot the Complication

0:10–0:15. Case fatality is 5–10%, higher in young children and when antitoxin is delayed.

Controlling a Diphtheria Outbreak

The Case
  1. Notify immediately (IDSP)
  2. Isolate until 2 negative cultures, 24 h apart
  3. Diphtheria antitoxin (ADS) without waiting for culture
  4. Penicillin or erythromycin; then complete immunisation
Contacts and Carriers
  1. Throat and nasal swabs of close contacts
  2. Antibiotic prophylaxis (benzathine penicillin or erythromycin for 7–10 days)
  3. Booster for immunised contacts; full course for the unimmunised
  4. Daily surveillance for 7 days; active case search

0:15–0:25. Antitoxin neutralises only unbound toxin, so give it early. Test dose for hypersensitivity per the product instructions. Older tests: Schick test (susceptibility to toxin), now of historic interest.

Epidemiology of Pertussis

AgentBordetella pertussis
SourceCases only (no carriers)
SpreadDroplets; very infectious (SAR up to 90%)
Incubation7–14 days (usually about 7–10)
InfectiousMost in the catarrhal stage; up to 3 weeks after paroxysms begin
Why Infants Die

Apnoea, pneumonia, seizures and encephalopathy. Maternal antibodies give little protection; most deaths are in infants too young to be fully vaccinated.

0:25–0:31. Adolescents and adults with waning immunity often have an atypical prolonged cough and infect infants at home.

The Three Stages of Pertussis

Treatment: azithromycin or erythromycin, best in the catarrhal stage; also for close contacts, especially infants.

0:31–0:38. The whoop is often absent in young infants, who may present with apnoea.

Take-Home

  1. Diphtheria: exotoxin, carriers, IP 2–6 days.
  2. Give antitoxin early; isolate; prophylaxis and vaccine for contacts.
  3. Pertussis: most infectious in the catarrhal stage; deadliest in infants.
  4. Pentavalent at 6, 10, 14 weeks; DPT boosters; Td at 10 and 16 years.
  5. Outbreaks mean missed boosters: check coverage.
