Classify the Reading

When systolic and diastolic fall in different categories, take the higher one.

Category

0:09–0:17. Park's table: optimal under 120 and under 80; normal under 130 and/or under 85; high normal 130–139 and/or 85–89; grade 1 140–159 and/or 90–99; grade 2 160 or more and/or 100 or more; hypertensive crisis 180 or more and/or 110 or more; isolated systolic hypertension 140 or more with diastolic under 90. Diagnosis needs repeated readings on separate occasions.

What Raises Blood Pressure?

0:27–0:35. Park cites Japan (sodium intake over 400 mmol a day, high hypertension) versus populations taking under 60 mmol a day with virtually no hypertension.

Measure It Right

  1. No tea, coffee, smoking or exercise for 30 minutes; empty bladder
  2. Sit 5 minutes, back supported, feet flat, arm at heart level
  3. Correct cuff size: a small cuff on a large arm over-reads
  4. Take two readings, 1–2 minutes apart; record the average
  5. Confirm on another day before labelling hypertension

0:35–0:41. Validated automated devices reduce observer bias and digit preference (readings ending in 0). Demonstrate with a student volunteer.

Prevent, Treat, Keep Them on Treatment

Population and Individual
  • Salt under 5 g a day
  • Healthy weight; 150 minutes of activity a week
  • Less alcohol; no tobacco
  • Screen everyone 30+ (NP-NCD)
India Hypertension Control Initiative (2017)
  • Simple standard treatment protocol
  • Reliable drug supply
  • Team-based care (nurses, CHOs)
  • Patient-centred follow-up; refills near home
  • Information system tracking control

0:41–0:52. IHCI began in 2017 with ICMR, MoHFW, WHO and state governments, and has been scaled up across states. Back to the case: a protocol, a refill at the Ayushman Arogya Mandir and a follow-up call would keep him on treatment. Check the current IHCI coverage.
