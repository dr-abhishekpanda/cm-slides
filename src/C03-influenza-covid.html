Influenza Viruses: A, B and C

Type A

Pandemics and Epidemics

Humans, birds, pigs. Subtyped by surface antigens: haemagglutinin (H) and neuraminidase (N), e.g. H1N1, H3N2, H5N1.

Type B

Epidemics

Humans only; no subtypes; milder, often in children.

Type C

Mild, Sporadic

Rarely causes significant disease.

H lets the virus enter cells; N lets new virus leave them (the target of oseltamivir).

0:03–0:07. Orthomyxovirus with a segmented RNA genome: segmentation is what makes reassortment (shift) possible.

Antigenic Drift vs Antigenic Shift

Drift

Small, Gradual Changes

Point mutations in H and N genes. Partial immunity remains. Causes yearly epidemics; why the vaccine is updated every year. Types A and B.

Shift

Sudden, Major Change

A new H (with or without N), usually by reassortment between human and animal viruses (pigs as "mixing vessels"). No immunity: pandemics. Type A only.

0:07–0:15. The 1918 pandemic killed an estimated 20–50 million people worldwide; India was among the worst hit.

Epidemiology of Seasonal Influenza

SpreadDroplets, aerosols, fomites
Incubation18–72 hours
Infectious1–2 days before to about 5 days after onset (longer in children)
SubclinicalCommon: silent spread
Season in IndiaPeaks in the monsoon and in winter (north)
High-risk Groups

Pregnant women; children under 5; adults over 65; chronic heart, lung, kidney or liver disease; diabetes; immunosuppression; obesity.

0:15–0:20. Short incubation, subclinical infection and many animal hosts make influenza impossible to eradicate.

India's A–B–C Categorisation

Category A

Mild fever, cough, sore throat, body ache.
No test, no oseltamivir; home isolation, symptomatic care; review in 24–48 h.

Category B

A + high fever and severe sore throat, or A in a high-risk person.
Oseltamivir, no test needed; home isolation.

Category C

Breathlessness, chest pain, drowsiness, low BP, haemoptysis, cyanosis; or worsening chronic disease.
Test, admit, oseltamivir.

0:20–0:30. Back to the opening case. Oseltamivir: 75 mg twice daily for 5 days in adults (weight-based in children). Check current NCDC guidance before teaching.

Prevention and Surveillance

Vaccine

Inactivated, given yearly. WHO picks strains twice a year (Northern and Southern Hemisphere). Priority: health workers, pregnant women, elderly, chronic illness.

Personal Measures

Cough etiquette, hand hygiene, masks, staying home when ill, ventilation

Surveillance

WHO's GISRS network; in India, ILI and SARI surveillance under IDSP/IHIP; NIV Pune and regional labs

0:30–0:35.

Avian Influenza (H5N1): The Pandemic We Fear

  • Spreads among wild birds and poultry; humans infected through close contact with sick or dead birds
  • Little sustained person-to-person spread, so far
  • Very high case fatality in reported human cases (about half)
  • Recent spread to mammals, including dairy cattle in the USA (2024)
Response in India

Culling and disinfection within a radius of the outbreak, surveillance zone, PPE and oseltamivir prophylaxis for cullers, ban on poultry movement, One Health coordination with animal husbandry.

0:35–0:42. India's culling radius and surveillance zone are set by the Department of Animal Husbandry action plan; check the current figures.

Take-Home

  1. Drift → yearly epidemics; shift → pandemics (type A only).
  2. Incubation 18–72 hours; many subclinical infections.
  3. Category A: no drug · B: oseltamivir · C: test and admit.
  4. Vaccinate yearly; protect pregnant women and the elderly.
  5. H5N1 and COVID-19 show why One Health surveillance matters.
