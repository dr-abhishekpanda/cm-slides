Why Breast Milk Wins

0:07–0:11.

Good Positioning: Four Points

  1. Baby's head and body in a straight line
  2. Baby held close to the mother's body
  3. Baby facing the breast, nose opposite the nipple
  4. Whole body supported (not just head and shoulders)

Holds: cradle, cross-cradle, football (underarm), side-lying. Use the one comfortable for mother and baby.

0:11–0:16. Demonstrate with a baby model and breast model (CM 5.12: DOAP). Cross-cradle suits newborns and small babies; football hold suits after caesarean section and twins.

Good Attachment, or Poor?

0:16–0:22. Poor attachment causes sore nipples, engorgement, low supply and an unsettled baby. Is the baby getting enough? At least 6 wet nappies a day and adequate weight gain.

Expressing and Storing Breast Milk

  1. Wash hands; use a clean wide-mouthed cup
  2. Thumb above, fingers below the areola (C-hold)
  3. Press back towards the chest, then compress and release
  4. Rotate around the areola; switch breasts when flow slows
  5. Feed by cup or paladai, never a bottle
Storage

Room temperature: about 6–8 hours
Refrigerator: about 24 hours
Do not boil or microwave; warm in a bowl of warm water.

Useful for working mothers, sick or low-birth-weight babies.

0:22–0:27. Demonstrate on the breast model. Storage times vary between guidelines; follow the one your state uses.

How Much, How Often, How Thick?

0:32–0:40. Katori = 250 ml bowl. Responsive feeding: feed patiently, encourage, never force; use a separate bowl for the child. Add a teaspoon of oil or ghee to increase energy density.

Diversity: At Least 5 of 8 Food Groups

Take-Home

  1. Breastfeed within 1 hour; no prelacteal feeds; colostrum is enough.
  2. Exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months; continue to 2 years.
  3. Check positioning (4 points) and attachment (4 signs).
  4. From 6 months: thick, frequent, varied food: 5 of 8 groups.
  5. Feed responsively; keep feeding during illness.
