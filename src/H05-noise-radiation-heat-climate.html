What Is Noise?

"Wrong sound, in the wrong place, at the wrong time."
Loudness

Decibels (dB)

A logarithmic scale: every 10 dB is a ten-fold rise in intensity. Reference: 0.0002 microbar.

Frequency

Hertz (Hz)

Human hearing: about 20–20,000 Hz. Below: infrasound. Above: ultrasound.

0:03–0:05. "Unwanted sound" is the common definition but subjective: one man's sound is another man's noise.

Climb the Decibel Ladder

Tap a rung

From Whisper to Rupture

Each rung shows how long you could safely listen, by the NIOSH rule: 85 dB(A) for 8 hours, halving for every 3 dB more.

0:05–0:10. Park: daily exposure up to 85 dB is about the limit without substantial hearing damage. Safe-time figures use the NIOSH 3-dB exchange rate; Indian factory rules differ, so present them as an illustration.

What Noise Does to Us

Auditory
  • Auditory fatigue: from about 90 dB, greatest at 4,000 Hz; tinnitus
  • Temporary hearing loss: 4,000–6,000 Hz, recovers within 24 h
  • Permanent loss: repeated exposure around 100 dB damages the organ of Corti
  • Above 160 dB: tympanic membrane may rupture
Non-auditory
  • Interference with speech (300–500 Hz)
  • Annoyance, irritability
  • Lower efficiency, more errors
  • Physiological: raised BP and heart rate, sweating, disturbed sleep

0:10–0:13. The audiogram notch at 4 kHz is the classic sign of noise-induced hearing loss; link to Occupational Health.

Controlling Noise

1

Plan Cities

Zoning; green belts; houses at least 15 m from the road; wide streets.

2

Control Vehicles

Heavy vehicles off narrow streets; ban pressure horns.

3

Insulate Buildings

Detached buildings; noisy machines isolated.

4

Protect People

Ear plugs and muffs; rotate workers; audiometry; legislation.

0:16–0:20. Hierarchy for occupational noise: control at source, in the path, then at the receiver (PPE last).

Radiation: The Hazard You Cannot Sense

Ionising and non-ionising, natural and man-made.

0:20.

Types and Sources of Radiation

Ionising
  • Corpuscular: alpha and beta particles, neutrons
  • Electromagnetic: X-rays and gamma rays
Non-ionising
  • Ultraviolet, visible, infrared, microwaves, radio waves
Sources
  • Natural: cosmic rays, terrestrial (radium, thorium; monazite sands of Kerala), radon, internal (potassium-40)
  • Man-made: medical X-rays and radiotherapy (the largest man-made share), nuclear power, fallout, industrial uses

0:20–0:23.

Measuring Radiation: Old and SI Units

0:23–0:25. Roentgen measures exposure (ionisation in air). Dose equivalent = absorbed dose × radiation weighting factor (alpha particles carry a high factor).

Biological Effects of Ionising Radiation

Somatic
ImmediateRadiation sickness; acute radiation syndrome
DelayedLeukaemia, cancers, foetal abnormalities, shortened life
Genetic
HeritableChromosome mutations; point mutations
High Doses
  • < 5 rad: no immediate effect
  • 5–50 rad: slight blood changes
  • 50–150 rad: blood changes, nausea, fatigue, vomiting
  • 150–1,100 rad: severe; deaths among some in 2+ weeks

0:25–0:29. The most radiosensitive tissues are rapidly dividing ones: bone marrow, gonads, gut lining, the foetus.

Principles

Justification, optimisation (ALARA: as low as reasonably achievable), dose limits. Personal dosimeters (TLD badges) for staff.

When Heat Kills

Heat illness, heat waves and a changing climate.

0:35.

From Mild to Lethal: Name the Heat Illness

Heat stroke: cool first, transport after. Every minute above 40 °C damages the brain.

0:36–0:42. Park describes heat hyperpyrexia/heat stroke as failure of heat regulation with very high body temperature. Field cooling: remove clothing, spray water and fan, ice packs to neck, axillae and groin; cold-water immersion where possible.

Preventing Heat Deaths

Individual
  • Drink water often, even without thirst; ORS
  • Avoid the sun from noon to 3 pm; light, loose cotton clothes
  • Acclimatise new workers over 1–2 weeks
  • Check on the elderly, infants and those with chronic disease
System
  • Heat Action Plans: city and district early warnings (first in Ahmedabad, 2013)
  • IMD heat-wave alerts; hospital preparedness; cool rooms
  • Shift outdoor work hours; shaded rest points
  • Surveillance of heat illness and deaths on the IHIP portal

0:42–0:47. Under the National Programme on Climate Change and Human Health (NPCCHH), districts report heat-related illness daily in summer. Local link: does your district have a Heat Action Plan?

Take-Home

  1. Noise is wrong sound, wrong place, wrong time; 85 dB a day is the safe ceiling.

  3. Radiation effects are somatic or genetic; protect by time, distance, shielding.
