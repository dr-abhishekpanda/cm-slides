The Sandfly
  • Tiny; hops rather than flies
  • Short flight range, low to the ground
  • Breeds in cracks of mud walls, damp organic soil, cattle sheds
  • Bites at dusk and night

0:03–0:10. Endemic belt: Bihar and Jharkhand contribute over 90% of cases; also West Bengal and Uttar Pradesh (633 endemic blocks in 54 districts by programme counts). Poverty, mud houses, cattle near homes, malnutrition and HIV co-infection raise risk.

PKDL: The Hidden Reservoir

  • Post kala-azar dermal leishmaniasis: skin lesions months to years after treatment
  • Hypopigmented macules, papules or nodules, often on the face
  • Painless, so people do not seek care
  • Lesions carry parasites that sandflies pick up
Why It Matters for Elimination

Active search for PKDL in every village that had kala-azar. Without it, transmission can restart even after cases fall.

0:25–0:33. Differentiate from leprosy and vitiligo: PKDL macules have normal sensation. Treatment: miltefosine (12 weeks) or liposomal amphotericin B per current guidelines.

Spraying Where the Sandfly Lives

  1. Indoor residual spraying, two rounds a year in endemic villages
  2. Synthetic pyrethroid (alpha-cypermethrin) replaced DDT after resistance
  3. Spray walls up to 6 feet and cattle sheds: sandflies rest low
  4. Plaster cracks; pucca houses (housing schemes) remove breeding sites

0:33–0:42. Quality of spraying (correct dose, full coverage of rooms) is monitored by squads. Community acceptance often drops when people repaint walls soon after spraying.
