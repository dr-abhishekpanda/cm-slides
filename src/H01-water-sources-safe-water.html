Where Does Our Water Come From?

Rain, surface water and ground water: three sources, very different risks.

0:12.

Rain Is the Prime Source of All Water

0:13–0:15. Ask the class to name each arrow before tapping. Rain is soft and corrodes lead pipes; it picks up dust, soot, microbes and gases on the way down. SO₂ and NOx from fossil fuels form acid rain.

Surface Water: Never Safe Without Protection and Purification

Impounding Reservoirs

Fairly Good

Artificial lakes behind dams. Clear, soft, next to rain water in purity. Keep the catchment area free of people and animals. Long storage → algae, bad taste.

Rivers and Streams

Grossly Polluted

Sewage, industrial and agricultural run-off, bathing, washing animals, disposal of the dead. Self-purification is not enough.

Tanks and Ponds

Highly Dangerous

Silt, weeds, washing, swimming and defecation at the edges. Yet often drunk untreated in villages.

"Rivers are described as a direct connection between the alimentary canal of the people living upstream and the mouths of those below."

0:15–0:18. "Clarity of water is no guarantee that the river water is safe for drinking." Sea water: 3.5% salts; desalination is costly.

Advantages of ground water: likely free of pathogens, usually needs no treatment, supply certain in dry season. Disadvantages: hard; needs pumping.

0:21–0:24. Most wells in India are shallow. Artesian wells: water rises above ground-water level under pressure between two impervious strata. Saline intrusion threatens wells near the sea. Step wells: personal contact with water, a hazard.

Tube Wells and Springs

Tube Wells

  • Bacteriologically safe and cheap
  • Shallow "driven wells": the largest single rural source
  • Pipe + strainer + hand pump + water-tight platform
  • Keep 15 m around free of liquid and solid waste
  • Usual life 5–10 years

Springs

  • Ground water reaching the surface under natural pressure
  • Shallow springs dry up in summer; deep springs do not
  • No pumping needed
  • Exposed to contamination: need protective structures

0:30–0:31. Deep (bored) tube wells are costly but in many ways the ideal supply.

When Water Makes Us Sick

Natural impurities, human pollution, and the diseases that follow.

0:31.

Pure Water Does Not Occur in Nature

Natural Impurities · Usually Harmless
  • Dissolved gases: N₂, CO₂, H₂S
  • Dissolved minerals: salts of Ca, Mg, Na
  • Suspended matter: clay, silt, sand, mud
  • Microscopic organisms
Human Activity · The Real Threat
  • Sewage: organic matter and pathogens
  • Industrial and trade wastes: metals to synthetic organics
  • Agricultural: fertilisers and pesticides
  • Physical: heat (thermal pollution), radioactivity

Even good water can be spoilt in the pipes: corrosion, leaky joints and cross-connections with sewers.

0:32–0:34. Indicators of pollution: suspended solids, BOD at 20°C, chlorides, nitrogen, phosphorus, absence of dissolved oxygen.

Not Every Water Problem Is a Germ

Fluoride

Two-edged

About 1 mg/L protects against caries. High levels cause dental and skeletal fluorosis. WHO guideline: 1.5 mg/L.

Nitrate

Blue Babies

Run-off from fertilised farmland → infant methaemoglobinaemia. WHO guideline: 50 mg/L as NO₃.

Arsenic

Ground Water

From minerals and ores. Skin lesions and cancers. WHO provisional guideline: 0.01 mg/L.

Hardness

A Surprise

Hard water shows an inverse association with cardiovascular deaths.

Concern today is less about acute poisoning and more about long-term, low-level exposure; some pollutants also accumulate in fish.

The Law: Water Act, 1974

The Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act gives a legal deterrent against water pollution.

  • Constituted the Central and State Pollution Control Boards (originally "Water Boards")
  • Boards have wide powers to sample, set standards and refuse consent to discharge

Take-Home

  1. Safe and wholesome: free of pathogens and harmful chemicals, pleasant, usable.
  2. Surface water is never safe without protection and purification.
  3. Ground water is safer; the deep well safest of wells.
  4. A well is sanitary only when it is covered and fitted with a hand pump.
  5. Clear water can still carry cholera, hepatitis, fluoride or arsenic.
