Food Fortification

Deliberately adding micronutrients to a widely eaten food to improve the nutritional quality of the food supply, with minimal risk to health.
Definition adapted from WHO/FAO
Why It Works

No change in habits; reaches everyone who eats the staple; cheap

The Vehicle Must Be

Eaten widely and regularly, centrally processed, and stable with the added nutrient

Limits

Misses those who don't buy processed food; risk of excess if poorly regulated

0:03–0:07. Classic examples: iodised salt (oldest), vitamin A and D in vanaspati (1953 in India).

India's +F Staples

Look for the +F logo. Fortified rice now reaches families through PDS, ICDS and PM-POSHAN.

0:07–0:15. The fortified-rice rollout was completed in phases by March 2024 (per Government of India releases); verify. People with thalassaemia and sickle cell disease were advised medical supervision regarding iron-fortified rice; check current FSSAI labelling advice.

Food Additives: Permitted, but Regulated

Preservatives

Benzoates, sulphites, sorbates

Colours

Natural (turmeric, saffron) and a short list of permitted synthetic colours, in limited amounts

Flavours and Sweeteners

MSG, artificial sweeteners (aspartame, sucralose)

Antioxidants

BHA, BHT: stop fats going rancid

Emulsifiers and Stabilisers

Lecithin, gums

The Rule

Only additives listed by FSSAI, within limits, declared on the label

0:15–0:23. Additives are deliberate and legal; adulterants are not. Each additive has an acceptable daily intake (ADI) set by JECFA.

Adulteration: Cheating That Can Kill

Common adulterants, home tests, and the law.

0:23.

Spot the Adulterant

Answer on your phone

0:23–0:31. DART home tests: starch in milk turns blue with iodine; papaya seeds float in alcohol while pepper sinks; brick powder in chilli settles fast in water. Demonstrate one test in class if possible.

From PFA to FSS Act

  • Prevention of Food Adulteration Act, 1954: the old law
  • Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006: consolidated eight food laws; science-based standards
  • FSSAI set up in 2008; licensing and registration of all food businesses
  • Food Safety Officers sample food; designated officers and adjudicating officers act
For the Public

Complain on the Food Safety Connect app or portal. Check the FSSAI licence number on every packet.

0:31–0:39. Penalties under the FSS Act range from fines to imprisonment (up to life imprisonment if adulteration causes death).

Take-Home

  1. Fortification reaches everyone who eats the staple: look for +F.
  2. Additives are legal if permitted, within limits and declared.
  3. Adulteration is illegal: the FSS Act, 2006 and FSSAI.
  4. Kesari dal → lathyrism; argemone → epidemic dropsy; mould → aflatoxin.
  5. Teach families simple home tests for adulteration.
