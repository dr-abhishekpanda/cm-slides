What Makes a Latrine Sanitary?

1

Excreta must not contaminate ground or surface water

2

Must not pollute the soil

3

Must not be accessible to flies, rodents, animals

4

Must not create a nuisance of odour or sight

15 m

Keep any latrine at least 15 m (50 ft) from a water source, and at a lower elevation.

0:13–0:15. Safe distance in practice depends on soil porosity, ground-water level, slope and direction of flow.

Swachh Bharat's Workhorse: The Twin-pit Toilet

  • Pour-flush pan with water seal; a junction chamber sends waste to one pit at a time
  • When pit 1 fills, switch to pit 2; pit 1 rests and its contents turn into safe, odourless manure
  • Empty the rested pit; no handling of fresh excreta
  • Honeycomb (leaching) walls let liquid soak away

0:22–0:25. Commonly cited design: each pit sized for about 3–5 years' use by a family of five; rest period of about 2 years before emptying. Verify the SBM(G) design norms you teach.

How a Septic Tank Works

  • 20–30 gallons (2–5 cu ft) per person; minimum 500 gallons
  • Length twice the breadth; depth 1.5–2 m
  • Retention period 24 hours
  • Stage 1: anaerobic digestion in the tank
  • Stage 2: aerobic oxidation of effluent in the soil (soakage pit)
  • Desludge periodically; never use the effluent as safe

0:25–0:33. Effluent still contains pathogens, ova and cysts; it must go to a soakage pit or drain field. Septic tanks are not recommended for large communities. Faecal sludge from septic tanks now goes to faecal sludge treatment plants (FSTPs) under SBM.

Measuring the Strength of Sewage

BOD

Biochemical Oxygen Demand

Oxygen used by bacteria to oxidise organic matter in 5 days at 20 °C. The higher the BOD, the stronger the sewage.

Also

COD, Suspended Solids

Chemical oxygen demand (all oxidisable matter) and suspended solids complete the picture.

20/30

Royal Commission (1908): effluent BOD ≤ 20 mg/L and suspended solids ≤ 30 mg/L, assuming 8:1 dilution in the river.

0:33–0:37. Current Indian discharge norms for STPs are set by CPCB/MoEFCC and are stricter for new plants; check the latest notification before quoting.

Inside a Sewage Treatment Plant

Primary treatment is physical; secondary treatment is biological (aerobic bacteria oxidise organic matter).

0:37–0:44. Activated sludge: sewage is aerated with a portion of returned "activated" sludge; the heart of the process is the aeration tank. Trickling filter: sewage sprinkled over a bed of stones coated with a zoogleal film.

Cheaper Alternatives

Oxidation Pond

Shallow pond; algae supply oxygen, bacteria oxidise organic matter. Cheap for small towns.

Oxidation Ditch

A mechanised, extended-aeration version of activated sludge.

Sewage Farming

About one acre per 100–300 persons; grow crops not eaten raw. Poorly managed: "sewage sickness".

Outfalls

Sea outfall into deep water. Raw sewage must never go into rivers.

Take-Home

  1. Faeces reach mouths by water, fingers, flies, fields and food.
  2. A sanitary latrine is the sanitation barrier; keep it 15 m from water.
  3. The water seal blocks flies and odour; twin pits make manure safely.
  4. Septic tank: 24-hour retention, anaerobic then aerobic purification.
  5. Sewage treatment: physical primary, biological secondary; aim for 20/30.
