The winning strategy was surveillance and containment: find every case, isolate it, and vaccinate every contact around it (ring vaccination).

0:03–0:08. Mass vaccination alone had failed in densely populated countries. Stocks of variola virus remain at two WHO-authorised laboratories (USA and Russia). Smallpox is still notifiable under IHR (2005).

Mpox: The Orthopoxvirus That Filled the Gap

  • A viral zoonosis (rodents, monkeys); now also spreads person to person, including sexual contact
  • Incubation: usually 6–13 days (range 5–21)
  • Fever, lymphadenopathy, then rash: face, palms, soles, genitals
  • Clades I (Central Africa, more severe) and II (West Africa)
Public Health Emergency

WHO declared mpox a PHEIC in July 2022 and again in August 2024 (clade Ib). Smallpox vaccination ended in 1980, so most people under 45 have no cross-protection.

0:14–0:20. Check the current PHEIC status before teaching: WHO ended the 2024 emergency in September 2025, but advice changes. India's guidelines: isolation till all lesions heal and scabs fall; contact tracing for 21 days.

Chickenpox: Mild, Common and Highly Infectious

Epidemiology, prevention and post-exposure prophylaxis.

0:26.

Epidemiology of Chickenpox

AgentVaricella-zoster virus (a herpesvirus)
ReservoirHumans only; latent in sensory ganglia (→ zoster)
TransmissionDroplets, airborne; contact with vesicle fluid
Incubation14–16 days (range 7–21)
Infectious1–2 days before rash until all lesions crust (about 5–6 days)
~90%secondary attack rate among susceptible household contacts

Worse in adults, pregnancy, newborns and the immunocompromised: pneumonia, encephalitis, secondary bacterial infection, congenital varicella syndrome.

0:26–0:32. Scabs are not infectious. Herpes zoster can transmit chickenpox to susceptible people, but not zoster.

Calculate the Secondary Attack Rate

SAR = new cases among contacts ÷ susceptible contacts × 100

Secondary Attack Rate

0:32–0:36. Exclude the primary case and immune contacts from the denominator; count only cases within one incubation period of the primary case.

Preventing Chickenpox

Vaccine

Live attenuated (Oka strain). Not in the UIP; IAP recommends 2 doses (15 months and 4–6 years). Contraindicated in pregnancy and immunodeficiency.

After Exposure

Vaccine within 3–5 days of exposure can prevent or modify disease in susceptible people.

High-risk Contacts

Varicella-zoster immune globulin (VZIG) for susceptible pregnant women, newborns and the immunocompromised, as early as possible (within 10 days).

Isolation: keep cases away from school or work until all lesions have crusted. Acyclovir for adults and high-risk patients.

0:36–0:44. Avoid aspirin in children with varicella (Reye's syndrome). Check current VZIG timing guidance.

Take-Home

  1. Smallpox was eradicated by surveillance and containment, declared in 1980.
  2. Mpox: zoonosis, lymphadenopathy, now spreading person to person.
  3. Chickenpox: centripetal, pleomorphic, superficial vesicles.
  4. Infectious from 1–2 days before rash until all lesions crust.
  5. Post-exposure: vaccine within 3–5 days; VZIG for high-risk contacts.
