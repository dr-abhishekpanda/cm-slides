Why Accumulated Refuse Is a Health Hazard

  1. Decomposes and favours fly breeding
  2. Attracts rodents and vermin
  3. Pathogens reach food through flies and dust
  4. Pollutes water and soil
  5. Unsightly heaps and bad odours
What Is Solid Waste?

Garbage (food waste), rubbish (paper, plastic, glass, metal), ash, demolition debris, street sweepings, sludge, dead animals, manure. Strictly, not nightsoil.

Output: 0.25–2.5 kg per person per day across countries.

Water held in discarded tyres, cups and containers breeds Aedes: improper refuse disposal is linked to vector-borne disease.

0:04–0:08. Sources (Park): street refuse, market refuse, stable litter, industrial refuse, domestic refuse (ash, rubbish, garbage). Garbage ferments and needs quick removal.

Storage and Collection

Storage

A Covered Bin

Galvanised steel bin with a close-fitting cover. Public bins on a concrete platform raised 2–3 inches. Capacity depends on users and frequency of collection.

Collection

House to House

"By far the best method". Recommended by the Environmental Hygiene Committee (1949). Now mandatory door-to-door collection of segregated waste under the SWM Rules 2016.

Replace open carts with enclosed vehicles. Where there is no door-to-door service, refuse ends up scattered along streets.

Exposed to flies and rodents

Leachate pollutes surface and ground water

0:12–0:15. Legacy dumps in Indian cities are now being "bio-mined" under SBM-Urban 2.0; fires at large dumps are a recurring air-pollution hazard.

Controlled Tipping (Sanitary Landfill)

  1. Dig a trench 2–3 m deep, 4–12 m wide
  2. Tip and compact refuse
  3. Cover with earth at the end of each day
  4. Repeat in layers; seal with at least 30 cm of earth
  5. Temperature exceeds 60 °C within 7 days; decomposition complete in 4–6 months

Three methods: trench (level ground), ramp (sloping), area (depressions, quarries). About one acre a year per 10,000 people at 2 m fill.

0:15–0:20. Park: "the most satisfactory method of refuse disposal where suitable land is available." Modified sanitary landfill = compaction and cover once or twice a week. Never tip refuse into water.

Incineration: Hygienic, but Not for Most Indian Refuse

Use When
  • No suitable land is available
  • Refuse is dangerous (e.g. some hospital waste)
  • Large cities in industrialised countries
Limits in India
  • High fine ash and moisture: burns poorly
  • Costly pre-separation and equipment
  • Loses manure value
  • Emissions need control

0:20–0:22. Modern equivalent: waste-to-energy plants, which need segregated, high-calorific waste and strict emission control.

Composting: The Bangalore Method (Anaerobic)

  1. Trench 90 cm deep, 1.5–2.5 m broad, 4.5–10 m long; at least 800 m from city limits
  2. Alternate 15 cm refuse and 5 cm nightsoil
  3. Heap rises 30 cm above ground; top layer refuse ≥ 25 cm
  4. Cover with excavated earth
  5. > 60 °C within 7 days, for 2–3 weeks; manure ready in 4–6 months

0:22–0:28. Developed at the Indian Institute of Science, Bangalore, under ICAR. Hot fermentation destroys pathogens and fly larvae. EHC (1949) did not recommend composting by municipalities over 100,000 population; they should have sewers.

Mechanical Composting, Manure Pits and Burial

Aerobic

Mechanical Composting

Salvage, pulverise to under 2 inches, mix with sludge, control C:N ratio, moisture, pH and aeration. Done in 4–6 weeks.

Rural

Manure Pits

Garbage, dung, straw and leaves into a pit; cover with earth daily. Two pits in rotation; manure in 5–6 months.

Camps

Burial

Trench 1.5 m wide, 2 m deep; cover each day's refuse with 20–30 cm of earth. When 40 cm from the top, fill with earth and dig a new trench.

0:28–0:32. Burial: a trench 1 m long per 200 persons fills in about a week; contents usable on fields after 4–6 months.

Solid Waste Management Rules, 2016

Every Waste Generator Must
  • Segregate into three streams: biodegradable (wet), non-biodegradable (dry), domestic hazardous
  • Hand over to authorised collectors; pay the user fee
  • Not burn, bury or throw waste in public places
Local Bodies Must
  • Collect segregated waste door to door
  • Process wet waste by composting or biomethanation
  • Send only non-usable, non-recyclable residue to sanitary landfill

The Rules cover urban areas, census towns, industrial townships, railways, airports, places of pilgrimage and more. Single-use plastic items were banned from 1 July 2022.

0:32–0:37. Bulk generators (e.g. hostels, large housing societies) must process wet waste on site where possible. Swachh Bharat Mission (Urban) 2.0 targets "Garbage Free" cities.

Take-Home

  1. Refuse breeds flies, rodents and mosquitoes, and pollutes water and soil.
  2. House-to-house collection of segregated waste is the standard.
  3. Open dumping is condemned; sanitary landfill covers refuse daily.
  4. Composting turns waste into manure; heat over 60 °C kills pathogens.
  5. Segregate at home into wet, dry and domestic hazardous.
