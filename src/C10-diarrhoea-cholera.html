Epidemiology of Cholera

AgentV. cholerae O1 (classical, El Tor; serotypes Ogawa, Inaba, Hikojima) and O139 (Bengal, 1992)
ReservoirCases and carriers (incubatory, convalescent, contact, chronic)
SpreadContaminated water and food
IncubationA few hours to 5 days; usually 1–2 days
Why El Tor Spreads

Many mild and inapparent infections for each severe case, longer carriage and better survival outside the body.

0:38–0:43. The seventh pandemic (from 1961) is due to El Tor, which spread from Sulawesi, Indonesia. Untreated severe cholera can kill in hours; with correct rehydration, case fatality should be under 1%.

Controlling a Cholera Outbreak

  1. Verify: stool culture (Cary-Blair transport), rapid tests; notify IDSP
  2. Set up treatment points; ORS for all, IV for severe
  3. Chlorinate water: residual 0.5 mg/L; boil-water advice
  4. Sanitation; safe disposal of stools and vomit
  1. Food hygiene; health education
  2. Oral cholera vaccine: 2 doses, 14 days apart
  3. Antibiotic for severe cases; no mass chemoprophylaxis
  4. Active case search; daily line list and epidemic curve

0:43–0:50. Single-dose doxycycline (300 mg in adults) or azithromycin for severe cases shortens illness and shedding. Chemoprophylaxis, if used, is limited to household contacts in selected settings. Quarantine and travel restrictions are not effective. An indigenous OCV (Hillchol) was launched in India in 2024.
