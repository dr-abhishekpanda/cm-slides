Obesity · Opening Case

"My BMI Is Normal, Doctor"

A 38-year-old office worker: BMI 24, waist 96 cm. He has just been told his sugar is in the prediabetes range.

Normal for whom? What did the BMI miss?

0:00–0:03. Asian Indians have more body fat and more abdominal fat at the same BMI. By Indian cut-offs, BMI 24 is overweight and a waist of 96 cm is abdominal obesity. Waist circumference is the better predictor of diabetes risk.

Obesity · Definition

What Is Obesity?

"an abnormal growth of the adipose tissue due to an enlargement of fat cell size (hypertrophic obesity) or an increase in fat cell number (hyperplastic obesity) or a combination of both"
Definition
Overweight Is Not Always Fat

Muscle or fluid can also raise weight; obesity means excess fat.

Where the Fat Is Matters

Abdominal (visceral) fat drives insulin resistance, diabetes and heart disease.

0:03–0:08. Other measures: skinfold thickness, bioelectrical impedance, DEXA (research). For field use: BMI and waist.

Park 27e, Ch 6: Obesity
Obesity · BMI Calculator

Work Out the BMI

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)². Example: 70 kg, 1.75 m → 22.9.

BMI, kg/m²
WHO

Indian Cut-offs

0:08–0:16. WHO: under 18.5 underweight; 18.5–24.9 normal; 25–29.9 overweight (pre-obese); 30 or more obese (Park adds a subdivision at 35–39.9). Indian consensus (2009): 18.0–22.9 normal; 23–24.9 overweight; 25 or more obese. WHO (2004) suggested 23 and 27.5 as public health action points for Asians.

WHO 2004 (Lancet); Misra et al., JAPI 2009 (Indian consensus)

Obesity · Waist

Measure the Waist

How

Midpoint between the lower border of the rib cage and the iliac crest, at the end of a normal breath.

0:16–0:23. Park: waist circumference correlates with BMI and WHR, is unrelated to height, and approximates intra-abdominal fat. Waist and WHR predict type 2 diabetes better than BMI.

Park 27e, Ch 6: Waist circumference and WHR; Misra et al., JAPI 2009

Obesity · Hazards

What Obesity Brings

Metabolic

Type 2 diabetes, dyslipidaemia, fatty liver, gallstones

Heart and Vessels

Hypertension, CHD, stroke

Others

Osteoarthritis, sleep apnoea, PCOS, some cancers, low self-esteem

0:37–0:42. Ask: which of these does your patient (the opening case) already show?

Obesity · Prevention

Prevention and Management

Population
  • Healthy school food; daily play and PE
  • Limit marketing of junk food to children
  • Walkable streets, parks, cycle lanes
  • Eat Right India; Fit India
Individual
  • Diet: modest calorie deficit; less sugar and fried food
  • Activity: 150–300 minutes a week
  • Behaviour: self-monitoring, sleep, family support
  • Drugs or surgery for severe obesity with complications

Losing just 5–10% of body weight improves BP, sugar and lipids.

0:42–0:55. Primordial prevention begins in childhood: breastfeeding, avoiding sugary drinks, active play. Weighing and measuring waist at every NCD screening makes obesity visible.
