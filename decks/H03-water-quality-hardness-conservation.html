Water III · Four Aspects of Quality

Four Aspects of Drinking-water Quality

I

Acceptability

Turbidity, colour, taste and odour, temperature; inorganic constituents

II

Microbiological

Bacteria, viruses, protozoa, helminths

III

Chemical

Inorganic and organic substances of health significance

IV

Radiological

Gross alpha 0.5 Bq/L; gross beta 1.0 Bq/L

Standards are exposure limits that carry legal force. India's national standard is IS 10500:2012 (BIS); WHO's guidelines are the basis countries build on.

0:04–0:06. Standards aim to minimise known hazards, since preventing all pollution is impossible.

Water III · Acceptability

The Consumer Judges Water by Eye, Nose and Tongue

Turbidity

> 4 NTU Is Visible

Interferes with disinfection and with microbiological tests.

Colour

Up to 15 TCU

Humic matter, iron, manganese, industrial waste.

Taste and Odour

No Guideline Value

A sudden change signals pollution or a treatment fault. Investigate.

Temperature

Cool Is Palatable

Warm water grows microbes and worsens taste, colour and corrosion.

Why it matters: unpleasant water drives people to less safe sources. Physical tests alone cannot judge safety.

Why Test for Coliforms and Not for Cholera Directly?

All coliforms are assumed faecal unless proved otherwise. Their presence means probable intestinal pathogens.

Water III · Three Indicators

Three Indicators, Three Messages

Coliforms / E. coli

Faecal Pollution

The primary indicator. E. coli confirms faecal origin.

Faecal Streptococci

Recent Pollution

Confirm doubtful cases. Resist drying: useful after laying new mains or repairs.

C. perfringens Spores

Remote Pollution

Survive long and resist usual chlorination. Present without coliforms → pollution at some remote time; in filtered water → faulty filtration.

Viruses: drinking water should be free of any virus infective for man. Free chlorine 0.5 mg/L for 1 hour at pH below 8 is considered sufficient.

Water III · Testing for Coliforms

Two Ways to Count Coliforms

Multiple Tube (MPN) Method
  1. Inoculate 0.1, 1, 10 and 50 mL into MacConkey's lactose bile salt broth (bromocresol purple)
  2. Incubate 48 h; tubes with acid and gas → presumptive coliform count (MPN/100 mL)
  3. Confirm in brilliant green bile broth at 37 °C (coliforms) and 44 °C (E. coli)
Membrane Filtration
  • Filter a measured volume through a cellulose-ester membrane
  • Incubate the membrane face up on medium; count colonies
  • Result in about 20 hours, against 72–96 hours for the tube method

Colony counts at 37 °C and 22 °C: a single count means little; a sudden rise in serial counts is the earliest warning of contamination.

0:22–0:26. E. coli is almost the only coliform producing gas from lactose at 44 °C; indole at 44 °C confirms. Field kits (H₂S strip tests) are used under Jal Jeevan Mission by trained village women.

Park 27e, Environment and Health: Bacteriological surveillance
Water III · Surveillance

Surveillance of Drinking-water Quality

  1. Sanitary survey: on-the-spot inspection of the whole supply system by a qualified person
  2. Sampling: "with the thoroughness of a surgical operation"
  3. Bacteriological examination
  4. Biological examination: algae, protozoa, crustaceans
  5. Chemical examination
In India Today

Jal Jeevan Mission

Five women per village trained to test water with field test kits; positive samples go to NABL-accredited district and state laboratories.

0:26–0:30. A sanitary survey is essential to interpret laboratory results. Verify current JJM water-quality numbers (labs, women trained) on ejalshakti.gov.in if you want to quote them.

Water III · Causes and Classes

How Hard Is This Water?

TypeCaused by
Carbonate ("temporary")Ca and Mg bicarbonates
Non-carbonate ("permanent")Ca and Mg sulphates, chlorides, nitrates

1 mEq/L of hardness = 50 mg/L as CaCO₃

0:30–0:34. Classes (Park): soft <1 mEq/L (<50 mg/L); moderately hard 1–3 (50–150); hard 3–6 (150–300); very hard >6 (>300). Drinking water should be moderately hard; soften above 3 mEq/L (150 mg/L).

Water III · Problems and Removal

Why Soften, and How?

Disadvantages
  • Wastes soap and detergent
  • Scaling of boilers: fuel loss, even explosions
  • Spoils cooking; shortens fabric life
  • Shortens life of pipes and fixtures; unsuitable for many industries
Removal
MethodRemoves
BoilingTemporary
Lime (Clark's method)Temporary
Sodium carbonateTemporary + permanent
Base exchange (permutit)Temporary + permanent, to zero

Zero-hardness water is corrosive: blend in some raw water. Areas with soft water show higher cardiovascular death rates (an inverse association).

0:34–0:40. Clark's method: one ounce of quick lime per 700 gallons for each degree (14.25 ppm) of hardness. Permutit is regenerated with brine.

Water III · Conservation

Ground Water Is Falling. Conserve It.

Extraction exceeds natural recharge around many cities and in tube-well farming belts.

  • Prevent wastage: awareness, efficient use in kitchen and bathroom, fix leaks
  • Water harvesting: collect rooftop rain to store or recharge
  • Recharge through pits, trenches, defunct wells and tube wells

0:40–0:46. Mention the Catch the Rain campaign (Jal Shakti Abhiyan) as a current national effort. First-flush diversion keeps the dirtiest rain out of storage.

Water III · Take-Home

Take-Home

  1. Quality has four aspects: acceptability, microbiological, chemical, radiological.
  2. India's standard: IS 10500:2012, with acceptable and permissible limits.
  3. E. coli must not be detectable in any 100 mL sample.
  4. Coliforms = faecal; streptococci = recent; C. perfringens = remote.
  5. Drinking water should be moderately hard; conserve every drop of ground water.
