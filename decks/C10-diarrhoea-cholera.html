How Much Fluid? Plans B and C

Plan B · ORS in 4 hours

75 mL/kg in the clinic, then reassess

Plan C · IV Ringer's Lactate, 100 mL/kg

Start ORS (5 mL/kg/h) as soon as the child can drink. Reassess every 1–2 hours.

0:20–0:28. Plan A: after each loose stool, 50–100 mL ORS under 2 years, 100–200 mL for 2–10 years; continue feeding; zinc for 14 days; return if danger signs. If weight is not known, the WHO age-based table can guide Plan B volumes.

Zinc for 14 Days

10 mg daily under 6 months

Zinc shortens the episode, reduces stool volume and lowers diarrhoea for 2–3 months after.

0:28–0:33. Grams per litre: sodium chloride 2.6, glucose anhydrous 13.5, potassium chloride 1.5, trisodium citrate dihydrate 2.9. Lower osmolarity reduced stool output, vomiting and need for IV fluids compared with the old 311 mOsm/L ORS. Antibiotics only for dysentery, suspected cholera with severe dehydration, or other specific infections.

Diarrhoea and Cholera · Prevention

Preventing Diarrhoea

Vaccines

Rotavirus at 6, 10, 14 weeks; measles-rubella; vitamin A

Feeding

Exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months; safe complementary food

WASH

Safe water, toilets, hand washing with soap at critical times

Campaign mode: Stop Diarrhoea Campaign (successor to the Intensified Diarrhoea Control Fortnight): ORS and zinc distribution by ASHAs before the monsoon.

Diarrhoea and Cholera · Cholera Epidemiology

Epidemiology of Cholera

AgentV. cholerae O1 (classical, El Tor; serotypes Ogawa, Inaba, Hikojima) and O139 (Bengal, 1992)
ReservoirCases and carriers (incubatory, convalescent, contact, chronic)
SpreadContaminated water and food
IncubationA few hours to 5 days; usually 1–2 days
Why El Tor Spreads

Many mild and inapparent infections for each severe case, longer carriage and better survival outside the body.

0:38–0:43. The seventh pandemic (from 1961) is due to El Tor, which spread from Sulawesi, Indonesia. Untreated severe cholera can kill in hours; with correct rehydration, case fatality should be under 1%.

Park 27e, Ch 5: Cholera
Diarrhoea and Cholera · Outbreak Control

Controlling a Cholera Outbreak

  1. Verify: stool culture (Cary-Blair transport), rapid tests; notify IDSP
  2. Set up treatment points; ORS for all, IV for severe
  3. Chlorinate water: residual 0.5 mg/L; boil-water advice
  4. Sanitation; safe disposal of stools and vomit
  1. Food hygiene; health education
  2. Oral cholera vaccine: 2 doses, 14 days apart
  3. Antibiotic for severe cases; no mass chemoprophylaxis
  4. Active case search; daily line list and epidemic curve

0:43–0:50. Single-dose doxycycline (300 mg in adults) or azithromycin for severe cases shortens illness and shedding. Chemoprophylaxis, if used, is limited to household contacts in selected settings. Quarantine and travel restrictions are not effective. An indigenous OCV (Hillchol) was launched in India in 2024.

Park 27e, Ch 5: Control of cholera; GTFCC

Diarrhoea and Cholera · Take-Home

Take-Home

  1. Classify dehydration: Plan A, B or C.
  2. Plan B: 75 mL/kg ORS in 4 hours.
  3. ORS 245 mOsm/L plus zinc for 14 days.
  4. Rotavirus vaccine, breastfeeding and WASH prevent it.
  5. Cholera outbreak: rehydrate, chlorinate, vaccinate; no mass prophylaxis.
