One Measles Case in a Tribal Residential School of 300 Children

Vaccination records show 70% received one dose of MR vaccine.

How many more cases do you expect, and what do you do today?

0:00–0:03. One dose gives about 85% protection; coverage of 70% leaves well over a hundred susceptible. Measles needs about 95% immunity to stop spread. Actions: isolate, vitamin A to cases, outbreak response immunisation of contacts within 72 hours, notify (IDSP), active case search.

Measles: The Most Infectious Disease We Know

AgentMeasles virus (paramyxovirus); one serotype
ReservoirHumans only; no carriers
SpreadDroplets and airborne; enters via respiratory tract and conjunctiva
IncubationAbout 10 days to fever; 14 days to rash
Infectious4 days before to 4 days after the rash
12–18basic reproduction number (R₀)
> 80%secondary attack rate among susceptible contacts

0:03–0:08. Measles does not occur subclinically in the unvaccinated, which helps surveillance. Peak age in India: 6 months to 3 years.

Why Measles Kills Children

Respiratory

Pneumonia (the commonest cause of death), otitis media, laryngotracheobronchitis

Gut and Nutrition

Diarrhoea; precipitates PEM and vitamin A deficiency (blindness)

Nervous System

Encephalitis; SSPE years later

Immune Amnesia

Measles wipes out existing immune memory for months to years

Treatment: vitamin A to every case (two doses, 24 hours apart), nutrition, treat complications.

0:14–0:20. Vitamin A doses by age: under 6 months 50,000 IU; 6–11 months 1 lakh IU; 12 months and above 2 lakh IU.

Measles–Rubella Vaccine in the UIP

Schedule

MR-1 at 9–12 months
MR-2 at 16–24 months
0.5 ml subcutaneous, right upper arm. Live vaccine: keep cold, protect from light, discard reconstituted vial after 4 hours.

In an Outbreak

Vaccine within 72 hours of exposure can prevent measles. Immunoglobulin within 6 days for contacts who cannot be vaccinated.

India aims to eliminate measles and rubella; MR campaigns since 2017 covered children 9 months to 15 years.

0:27–0:33. Check the current national elimination target year (it has been revised more than once). Reconstituted measles vaccine left warm has caused deaths from toxic shock (contamination): a key safety teaching point.

Measles, Rubella, Mumps · Rubella and CRS

Rubella: Mild in the Child, Cruel to the Foetus

  • Togavirus; incubation 2–3 weeks (about 18 days)
  • Mild fever, rash, post-auricular and sub-occipital lymphadenopathy
  • Infectious about a week before to a week after the rash

0:33–0:42. Classic CRS triad: cataract, sensorineural deafness, congenital heart disease (PDA). Also microcephaly, mental retardation, hepatosplenomegaly, "blueberry muffin" rash. Risk figures on the slide are approximate teaching values from Park and WHO; the earlier the infection, the higher the risk. Avoid pregnancy for 4 weeks after rubella vaccination.

Mumps

AgentMumps virus (paramyxovirus)
SpreadDroplets, saliva
IncubationAbout 18 days (14–25)
InfectiousSeveral days before to about a week after parotid swelling
SubclinicalUp to a third of infections
Complications

Orchitis in about a quarter of post-pubertal males (sterility is rare), meningitis, pancreatitis, oophoritis, deafness.

MMR is not in the UIP; IAP recommends it privately.

Take-Home

  1. Measles: R₀ 12–18; stop it with about 95% immunity.
  2. Infectious 4 days before to 4 days after rash; Koplik's spots come first.
  3. Give vitamin A to every measles case.
  4. MR vaccine at 9–12 and 16–24 months; outbreak dose within 72 hours.
  5. Rubella's danger is CRS: cataract, deafness, heart defects.
