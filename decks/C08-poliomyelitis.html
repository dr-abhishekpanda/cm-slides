Poliomyelitis · Epidemiology

Poliovirus: The Agent and Its Route

AgentEnterovirus; types 1, 2, 3. Type 1 causes most paralysis and outbreaks
ReservoirHumans only; mostly inapparent infections
SpreadFaecal-oral (main route in India); droplets early
IncubationUsually 7–14 days (range 3–35)
Infectious7–10 days before and after onset; faeces for 3–6 weeks
Why Eradication Is Possible

No animal reservoir, no long-term carrier state, effective vaccines, and immunity that is type-specific and lifelong.

0:03–0:08. Host factors that raise paralysis risk: intramuscular injections, tonsillectomy and heavy exertion during the incubation period ("provocation paralysis"). Most infections occur in children under 5 in endemic settings.

Poliomyelitis · Vaccine-derived Virus

When the Vaccine Virus Changes

Most cVDPV outbreaks worldwide have been type 2, which is why type 2 was removed from OPV in 2016.

0:21–0:25. The solution to cVDPV is not to stop vaccinating but to vaccinate everyone, so the vaccine virus has no one left to circulate in. Novel OPV2 (nOPV2) is a genetically more stable vaccine used in outbreak response.

Poliomyelitis · AFP Case Response

From One AFP Case to Lab Result

Case Definition

Any child under 15 years with acute flaccid paralysis, or any person of any age with paralytic illness if polio is suspected.

Report These Too

Guillain-Barré syndrome, traumatic neuritis and transverse myelitis are all reported as AFP first.

  1. Report immediately; investigate within 48 hours
  2. Two stool samples, 24 hours apart, within 14 days of onset
  3. Reverse cold chain (2–8 °C) to a WHO-accredited lab
  4. Search for more cases in the area
  5. Follow-up at 60 days for residual paralysis

0:37–0:45. Environmental (sewage) surveillance supplements AFP surveillance and can detect virus with no paralysed case.

Poliomyelitis · Is Surveillance Good Enough?

Is This District's Surveillance Good Enough?

Non-polio AFP Rate

0:45–0:50. Targets used in India: non-polio AFP rate at least 2 per 100,000 children under 15 (WHO global minimum is 1), and adequate stool collection in at least 80% of cases. A low rate means cases are being missed, not that the district is healthy.

Poliomyelitis · Take-Home

Take-Home

  1. Faecal-oral; 90–95% inapparent; under 1% paralytic.
  2. India's last case: 13 January 2011; certified 2014.
  3. bOPV for the gut, fIPV (6 weeks, 14 weeks, 9 months) for the blood.
  4. Every AFP under 15: 2 stools, 24 h apart, within 14 days.
  5. NP-AFP rate ≥2 per lakh; adequate stools ≥80%.
