Biomedical Waste · Definition

What Is Biomedical Waste?

"Any waste, which is generated during the diagnosis, treatment or immunization of human beings or animals or in research activities pertaining thereto or in the production or testing of biological or in health camps."
Bio-Medical Waste Management Rules, 2016
75–90%General (non-risk) waste, comparable to domestic waste
10–25%Hazardous: infectious, sharps, chemical, pharmaceutical, cytotoxic, radioactive

0:03–0:06. Good segregation keeps the hazardous fraction small; mixing turns all of it hazardous. WHO planning estimates: 80% general, 15% pathological and infectious, 1% sharps, 3% chemical and pharmaceutical, under 1% special.

Park 28e, Ch 16: Hospital Waste Management; BMW Management Rules 2016, Rule 3
Biomedical Waste · Who Is at Risk

Hazards: Who Gets Hurt, and How?

Hazardous Because It
  • Contains infectious agents (HIV, HBV, HCV; resistant bacteria)
  • Contains sharps
  • Is toxic, chemical or pharmaceutical
  • Is genotoxic (cytotoxic drugs)
  • Is radioactive
At Risk
  • Doctors, nurses, auxiliaries, maintenance staff
  • Patients and visitors
  • Laundry, transport and waste-handling workers
  • Workers at landfills and incinerators; ragpickers

The public is also sensitive to the sight of healthcare waste, especially anatomical waste.

Biomedical Waste · Four Colours

Four Colours, Four Fates

Yellow

Burn

Anatomical, soiled dressings, expired medicines, chemical waste, contaminated linen, lab waste (pre-treated)

Incineration / plasma pyrolysis / deep burial*

Red

Recycle

Contaminated recyclable plastic: tubing, IV sets, catheters, urine bags, syringes without needles, gloves

Autoclave/microwave + shred → recycler

White (translucent)

Sharps

Needles, syringes with fixed needles, scalpels, blades

Puncture-, leak- and tamper-proof container; autoclave, shred, encapsulate or sharps pit

Blue

Glass

Broken or discarded contaminated glass, vials, ampoules; metallic body implants

Cardboard box with blue marking; disinfect → recycle

* Deep burial only in rural or remote areas with no access to a common treatment facility, with prior approval. Bags must be non-chlorinated.

0:11–0:18. Cytotoxic drugs and items contaminated with them go in yellow bags marked cytotoxic, returned to the manufacturer or incinerated above 1200 °C. Glass contaminated with cytotoxic drugs goes to yellow, not blue.

BMW Management Rules 2016, Schedule I (as amended 2018)

Biomedical Waste · Incineration

Incineration

High-temperature dry oxidation that reduces organic waste to inorganic ash and cuts volume and weight sharply.

Primary chamber800 ± 50 °C
Secondary chamber1050 ± 50 °C
Gas residence timeat least 2 seconds
Never Incinerate
  • Pressurised gas containers
  • Large amounts of reactive chemicals
  • Silver salts, photographic and X-ray waste
  • Halogenated plastics such as PVC
  • Mercury or cadmium waste (thermometers, batteries)
  • Sealed ampoules or ampoules of heavy metals

0:31–0:36. Types: double-chamber pyrolytic, single-chamber static grate, and rotary kiln (decomposes genotoxic and heat-resistant chemicals). Burning PVC releases dioxins and furans: one reason chlorinated plastic bags and gloves were phased out under the 2016 Rules.

Park 28e, Ch 16: Incineration; BMW Rules 2016, Schedule II (emission standards)

Biomedical Waste · Key Duties under the Rules

The Occupier's Duties: Key Points

  • Segregate at source; pre-treat lab, microbiology and blood-bag waste on site (autoclave/microwave)
  • Phase out chlorinated plastic bags and gloves (by 27 March 2019)
  • Bar-code all bags and containers
  • Hand over to a CBWTF; on-site incinerators only where no CBWTF is available
  • Train all health workers; immunise them against hepatitis B and tetanus
  • Keep records for 5 years; submit an annual report to the SPCB by 30 June
  • Report major accidents; display monthly records on the website
  • Set up a BMW management committee (for larger hospitals)

0:42–0:48. Verify the latest amendments on the CPCB website before teaching (e.g. any changes after 2019).

BMW Management Rules 2016 (amended 2018, 2019): Rule 4 duties of the occupier

Biomedical Waste · Take-Home

Take-Home

  1. Only 10–25% of hospital waste is hazardous: segregation keeps it that way.
  2. Yellow burn · Red recycle · White sharps · Blue glass.
  3. Pre-treat lab waste on site; treat all BMW within 48 hours.
  4. Never incinerate PVC, mercury or pressurised containers.
  5. Train and immunise every health worker; report needle-stick injuries at once.

0:55–0:60. Practical: visit the hospital's BMW storage area and CBWTF if possible.
