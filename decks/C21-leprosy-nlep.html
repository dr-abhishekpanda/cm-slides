Leprosy · Opening Case

A Pale Patch That Does Not Feel

A 14-year-old with a hypopigmented patch on the back for a year. It does not itch. When touched with a wisp of cotton, she cannot feel it.

Is this leprosy? And what does a child case
tell you about her village?

0:00–0:03. A hypopigmented patch with definite loss of sensation is a cardinal sign. A child case means recent, active transmission in the community: examine all household contacts and give single-dose rifampicin to eligible contacts.

What Each Indicator Tells You
  • Child proportion: recent transmission

0:03–0:10. India achieved elimination as a public health problem (prevalence below 1 per 10,000) nationally in December 2005 and still reports over half the world's new cases. Highest prevalence in 2024–25: Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Odisha, Chandigarh and Maharashtra. Child-case and disability percentages differ slightly between PIB and other sources.

Leprosy · Epidemiology

How Leprosy Spreads

AgentMycobacterium leprae; grows very slowly; prefers cooler tissues: skin and nerves
SourceUntreated MB patients; nasal discharge
SpreadDroplets during prolonged close contact; possibly skin
IncubationLong: average 3–5 years (months to 20 years)
Most People Are Resistant

Only a small fraction of exposed people develop disease. One month of MDT makes a patient non-infectious.

0:10–0:17. Household contacts of MB cases carry the highest risk. Leprosy is not hereditary and not spread by touch in casual contact; say this explicitly to fight stigma.

Leprosy · Cardinal Signs

The Cardinal Signs

Any one sign makes a case. Nerves to feel: ulnar, radial cutaneous, common peroneal, posterior tibial, great auricular.

0:17–0:22. Demonstrate testing sensation with a wisp of cotton or a ballpoint pen tip, with the patient's eyes closed.

Leprosy · Disability

Prevent Disability

Grade 0

No anaesthesia, no visible deformity

Grade 1

Loss of sensation in hands or feet; no visible damage

Grade 2

Visible deformity: claw hand, foot drop, ulcers, lagophthalmos

Reactions (type 1 and ENL) and neuritis need prompt steroids. Self-care of anaesthetic hands, feet and eyes; footwear; reconstructive surgery.

0:38–0:45. Grade 2 disability rate per million population is the key impact indicator; the global target is zero grade 2 disability in new child cases.

National Leprosy Eradication Programme

Goal of the National Strategic Plan 2023–27: zero transmission of leprosy at sub-national level by 2027.

Leprosy · Take-Home

Take-Home

  1. Patch with loss of sensation: think leprosy.
  2. PB 1–5 lesions; MB 6+, more than 1 nerve, or smear +.
  3. Three drugs: PB 6 months, MB 12 months.
  4. Child cases = transmission; grade 2 = late detection.
  5. Contacts get single-dose rifampicin.
