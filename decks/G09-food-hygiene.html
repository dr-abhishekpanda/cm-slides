Food Hygiene · Testing Milk

Was It Pasteurised Properly?

Phosphatase Test

Raw milk contains phosphatase, destroyed at the time–temperature that pasteurisation needs. A negative test means pasteurisation was efficient; it also detects raw milk added later.

Other Tests
  • Standard plate count: bacterial load
  • Coliform count: contamination after pasteurisation
  • Methylene blue reduction: keeping quality

0:14–0:20. Clean milk production: healthy animals, clean udders and hands, clean utensils, cooling quickly, covered containers.

Food Hygiene · Meat and Other Foods

Meat, Fish, Eggs and Vegetables

Meat Hygiene
  • Licensed slaughterhouses; ante- and post-mortem inspection
  • Diseases: Taenia (tapeworms), trichinosis, anthrax, salmonellosis, toxoplasmosis
  • Cook thoroughly; refrigerate
Other Foods
  • Fish: fresh fish has bright eyes, red gills, firm flesh
  • Eggs: Salmonella; avoid raw or half-cooked eggs
  • Raw vegetables and fruits: wash in safe water; sewage-farm produce eaten raw is risky

Food Hygiene · Food Handlers

Food Handlers: The Weakest Link

Medical Examination

At hiring and periodically; stool tests for carriers (typhoid, amoebiasis); exclude those with diarrhoea, skin infections, sore throat

Personal Hygiene

Handwashing with soap; short clean nails; covered hair; no smoking or spitting; cover cuts

Training

FSSAI's FoSTaC training for food-safety supervisors; regular health education

Famous lesson: "Typhoid Mary", a healthy cook who infected dozens of people.

Food Hygiene · Investigating an Outbreak

Investigating the Wedding Outbreak

  1. Confirm the outbreak; list all who ate, ill and well
  2. Case definition; symptoms; time of onset → incubation period
  3. Collect food leftovers, vomit and stool early
  4. Calculate food-specific attack rates
  5. Inspect the kitchen and food handlers
  6. Control, report on IDSP/IHIP, and educate

Food Hygiene · Take-Home

Take-Home

  1. Milk can carry disease from the animal or the handler.
  2. Pasteurise: 63–66 °C × 30 min, or 72 °C × 15 s; check with the phosphatase test.
  3. Examine and train food handlers; look for carriers.
  4. Short incubation (1–6 h) = preformed toxin.
  5. Find the vehicle with food-specific attack rates.
