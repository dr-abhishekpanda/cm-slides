Viral Hepatitis · Hepatitis A and E

The Enteric Pair: A and E

Hepatitis A
  • Mostly children, often without jaundice
  • Infection in childhood gives lifelong immunity
  • As sanitation improves, infection shifts to adults, who get sicker
  • Virus in faeces 1–2 weeks before jaundice
Hepatitis E
  • Water-borne epidemics in young adults
  • Delhi, 1955–56: about 29,000 cases after sewage entered the water supply
  • Pregnancy, third trimester: fatality up to 20%
  • No specific vaccine in India

Control for both: safe water, sanitation, hand washing, and safe food handling.

0:10–0:18. The Delhi outbreak was first thought to be hepatitis A; stored sera later showed it was hepatitis E. Outbreak response: find and fix the leak, chlorinate (residual chlorine 0.5 mg/L), boil-water advice, active case search, and special watch on pregnant women.

The Silent Chronic Burden

Hepatitis B and C: cirrhosis and liver cancer decades later.

0:18. NVHCP estimates about 40 million people in India live with chronic hepatitis B and 6–12 million with hepatitis C. Hepatitis B: intermediate endemicity (HBsAg about 2–8% in the general population; studies vary).

Viral Hepatitis · Prevention

Preventing Hepatitis B and C

Vaccine

Birth dose within 24 hours, then pentavalent at 6, 10, 14 weeks. Health workers: 0, 1, 6 months.

Newborn of HBsAg+ Mother

Birth dose plus HBIG soon after birth; complete the series.

Safe Blood and Needles

Screen all blood; AD syringes; no reuse; standard precautions; harm reduction for people who inject drugs.

No vaccine exists for hepatitis C: prevention rests on safe injections and blood.

0:37–0:44. NVHCP recommends HBsAg screening of pregnant women so that exposed newborns receive HBIG plus vaccine. Post-exposure for health workers: HBIG plus vaccine if unvaccinated or non-responder.

MoHFW UIP; NVHCP Operational Guidelines 2018
Viral Hepatitis · NVHCP

National Viral Hepatitis Control Programme

  • Launched 28 July 2018 (World Hepatitis Day)
  • Goal: end viral hepatitis as a public health threat by 2030 (SDG 3.3)
  • Free diagnosis and treatment: hepatitis C cured by 12 weeks of direct-acting antivirals; hepatitis B managed long term
  • Model treatment centres and treatment sites in districts
  • Awareness, safe injections, birth-dose coverage
Prevention + Cure

Hepatitis C can be cured. Hepatitis B can be prevented. Both need people to be tested.

0:44–0:50. DAA regimens such as sofosbuvir with daclatasvir or velpatasvir. Check the current NVHCP technical guidelines for regimens and eligibility.

Take-Home

  3. B, C, D: blood, sex, mother to baby; often chronic.
