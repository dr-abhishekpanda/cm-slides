Mosquitoes · How Arthropods Transmit Disease

How Arthropods Transmit Disease

1

Direct Contact

Transferred person to person: scabies mite, lice.

2

Mechanical

Carried on legs or body, no development: housefly and typhoid, cholera, trachoma.

3

Biological

The agent multiplies or develops inside the vector, which then needs an extrinsic incubation period.

Biological typeWhat happens in the vectorExample
PropagativeMultiplies, no change in formPlague bacilli in rat flea
Cyclo-propagativeChanges form and multipliesMalaria parasite in Anopheles
Cyclo-developmentalChanges form, no multiplicationFilarial larvae in Culex

0:03–0:10. A classic viva question. Extrinsic incubation period: time from ingestion by the vector until it becomes infective (e.g. about 10–14 days for P. vivax in Anopheles, depending on temperature).

Mosquitoes · Adults

Adults: Posture, Palps and Patterns

  • Anopheles: rests at an angle; female palps as long as the proboscis; wings usually spotted
  • Culex: rests parallel; female palps short; plain wings; night biter
  • Aedes: black with white stripes on body and legs ("tiger mosquito"); day biter
  • Mansonia: speckled wings and legs; breeds where water plants (Pistia) grow

Mosquitoes · Control at a Glance

Mosquito Control at a Glance

Anti-larval
  • Environmental: source reduction, drainage, filling, covering tanks
  • Chemical: temephos, larvicidal oil
  • Biological: larvivorous fish (Gambusia, guppy), Bti
Anti-adult
  • Indoor residual spraying
  • Space spraying (fogging) in outbreaks
  • Genetic and sterile-insect methods (research)
Personal Protection
  • LLINs (long-lasting insecticidal nets)
  • Repellents, screens, full sleeves
  • Community: dry day every week

Details of insecticides and Integrated Vector Management come in the next lecture.

0:44–0:48. Aedes control is mainly source reduction: empty and scrub containers weekly ("dry day"), because Aedes breeds in clean, stored water.

Mosquitoes · Take-Home

Take-Home

  1. Biological transmission: propagative, cyclo-propagative, cyclo-developmental.
  2. Egg, larva and pupa live in water: no water, no mosquito.
  3. Anopheles: no siphon, rests at an angle, spotted wings, long palps.
  4. Aedes: striped, day-biting, breeds in containers of clean water.
  5. Control the larva first: source reduction beats spraying.
