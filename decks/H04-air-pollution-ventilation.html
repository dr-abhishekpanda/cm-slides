Air · What We Breathe

What Is in a Breath of Clean Air?

78%■ Nitrogen
21%■ Oxygen (20.93%)
~1%■ Argon and others
0.04%Carbon dioxide, rising

In a crowded room, O₂ falls, CO₂, heat and humidity rise, and body odour builds. Discomfort comes mainly from heat and humidity, not CO₂.

0:03–0:05. Park notes that even CO₂ above 5% and O₂ at 18% caused no harm so long as the "cooling power" of air was satisfactory; that is why air change matters more than cubic space.

Air · Definition and Sources

Four Sources of Air Pollution

a

Automobiles

Hydrocarbons, CO, lead, NOₓ, particulates. Sunlight turns some into photochemical oxidants.

b

Industries

Smoke, SO₂, NOₓ, fly ash; HF, HCl, organic halides from petrochemicals.

c

Domestic Sources

Coal, wood, dung and crop waste in homes: smoke, dust, SO₂, NOₓ.

d

Miscellaneous

Burning refuse, incinerators, pesticide spraying, natural dust, nuclear fallout.

More than 100 air pollutants are known. Key ones: CO, CO₂, SO₂, NOₓ, H₂S, fluorides, hydrocarbons, metals (lead, arsenic, cadmium), ozone and particulate matter.

0:05–0:09. Add stubble burning as a seasonal source in north India and construction dust in growing towns.

Air · Temperature Inversion

Why Winter Mornings Are the Worst: Temperature Inversion

Normally air cools with height. Warm polluted air rises and disperses.

0:12–0:15. Park: rapid cooling of lower layers gives little vertical motion; pollutants and water vapour are trapped low, giving smog; commoner in winter. London smog, December 1952: about 4,000 excess deaths.

Air · Health Effects

What Air Pollution Does

Immediate

Acute

Irritation of eyes and airways, acute respiratory infections, asthma attacks, excess deaths in smog episodes. CO: carboxyhaemoglobin.

Delayed

Chronic

Chronic bronchitis, COPD, lung cancer, heart disease, stroke; lead in children.

Beyond Health

Social and Economic

Damage to vegetation and animals, corrosion of buildings and metals, soiling, reduced visibility, acid rain.

0:15–0:20. Ask: which groups are most vulnerable? Children, elderly, pregnant women, those with heart or lung disease, outdoor workers.

Air · Indoor Air

Back to the Two Kitchens: Household Air Pollution

  • Burning wood, dung, crop residue or coal in poorly ventilated homes
  • Women and young children bear the highest exposure
  • Linked to childhood pneumonia, COPD, lung cancer, cataract and low birth weight
  • Remedies: clean fuel (LPG, electricity), improved stoves, a chimney, a separate, ventilated kitchen
India

Pradhan Mantri Ujjwala Yojana

Free LPG connections for women in poor households since 2016. Refill use is the real test.

Air changes/hour = hourly air supply ÷ room volume

0:36–0:42. Park's standards: living rooms 2–3 air changes/hour; workrooms and assembly halls 4–6; more than 6 gives draught. Cubic space 1,000–1,200 cu ft per person is sufficient. Floor space 50–100 sq ft per person. Heights over 10–12 ft are not counted.

Air · Types of Ventilation

Natural or Mechanical?

Natural
  • Wind: perflation (blowing through), aspiration (suction behind an obstruction)
  • Cross-ventilation: doors and windows facing each other. Back-to-back houses are not allowed.
  • Diffusion: slow; never the sole means
  • Inequality of temperature: cool air in low, warm air out high

Drawback: velocity, temperature and humidity cannot be regulated.

Mechanical
  • Exhaust: fans extract air (kitchens, factories)
  • Plenum: fresh air blown in
  • Balanced: plenum + exhaust together
  • Air conditioning: controls temperature, humidity, purity and movement

0:42–0:46. Mention sick building syndrome as a hazard of sealed, poorly maintained air-conditioned buildings.

Air · Thermal Comfort

Thermal Comfort: Corrected Effective Temperature

Comfort depends on air temperature, humidity, air movement and radiant heat. Comfortable thermal conditions let a person keep heat balance at normal body temperature without sweating.

Air · Take-Home

Take-Home

  1. Automobiles, industry, homes and miscellaneous burning pollute our air.
  2. PM2.5 is the pollutant that matters most for health.
  3. Temperature inversion traps pollution near the ground.
  4. Control: containment, replacement, dilution, legislation, international action.
  5. Air change beats cubic space: 2–3 per hour at home, 4–6 at work.
